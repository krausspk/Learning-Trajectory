Scope of the Supplementary Information
This document reports the analyses that support, qualify, or extend the main results. Pair values are
displayed to show the full structure of the data, but they are not treated as independent observations
because each network occurs in multiple pairs. Unless stated otherwise, confidence intervals were
therefore calculated by deleting complete training runs within dataset strata. The supplementary
analyses describe dataset- and task-dependent organization of weights and representations; they do
not establish an identical learned algorithm, a dynamical attractor, or an evolutionary process.


S1      Training cohort and diagnostics
Test accuracy and cross-entropy loss were read from the stored evaluation history of every net-
work at every checkpoint. Thin trajectories represent individual training runs; thick trajectories are
dataset means. Final-checkpoint distributions retain the complete run-level variation and are shown
descriptively without treating checkpoints as independent observations.
    All 30 networks learned their classification problem, and no run showed a failed trajectory. At the
final checkpoint, mean test accuracy was 97.37% for MNIST, 86.37% for Fashion-MNIST, and 85.99%
for KMNIST (Table S1). Variation among the ten runs in each group remained small. KMNIST
provided the only notable qualification: its accuracy stabilized while cross-entropy loss increased
late in training, consistent with increasing confidence on the remaining errors rather than declining
classification accuracy.

S2     Full layer-by-metric trajectories
For each checkpoint, Euclidean dissimilarity, SND, and raw Alpha were summarized separately for
W0 , W1 , the equal-weighted mean of both layers, and the parameter-count-weighted mean. Within-
and between-dataset estimates and their confidence intervals were obtained with the dataset-stratified
delete-one-run jackknife defined in the main Methods.
    Every layer-by-measure contrast was positive at E20 (Table S2). The relative size of the layer
effects nevertheless depended strongly on the measure. Euclidean dissimilarity and SND gave larger
absolute contrasts in W1 , whereas raw Alpha gave a much larger contrast in W0 . Parameter-count
weighting assigned 98.74% of the aggregate to W0 and therefore closely followed that layer. The robust
conclusion is the direction of the within-/between-dataset separation; claims about the magnitude
or geometry of the separation remain measure-dependent.

S3                                  Run-level distributions and robustness
For each network at the final checkpoint, we calculated its mean dissimilarity to the other runs
from the same dataset and to all runs from the other datasets. Their difference is the run-level
contribution to the global between-minus-within contrast. These contributions expose whether an
aggregate result is driven by a small subset of networks.
    All 30 run contributions were positive for Alpha in both layers, and the same direction held for
every plotted measure and aggregation. The contributions varied in size, particularly across datasets
for W0 , but no isolated run determined the pooled result. The plotted points are useful for showing
replication across complete runs; inferential uncertainty still comes from the stratified jackknife rather
than from treating the 435 shared-network pairs as independent samples.

S4     Task-control details
The paired task control used the same MNIST images, identical input-layer initialization within each
seed block, and the same image order, while changing only the prediction rule between digit identity
and parity. Uncertainty was estimated by deleting an entire paired seed block. Group-specific
endpoint means distinguish within-identity, within-parity, same-seed cross-task, and different-seed
cross-task comparisons.
    Both tasks were learned reliably, reaching mean final accuracies of 97.25% for identity and 98.60%
for parity. Although the paired networks began with bitwise identical W0 matrices, training produced
positive between-task contrasts under all three weight measures and CKA (Table S3). The same-
seed cross-task Alpha mean was 0.07857, close to 0.08153 for cross-task comparisons with different
seeds. The task effect therefore cannot be explained by different random initializations. Alpha uses
raw W0 and incoming-weight-only Hungarian assignment; the implemented whole-network Git Re-
Basin objective is not directly applicable across the different output dimensions. Because the output
dimensions differed, this control supports a target-rule effect for W0 and the hidden representation,
not a whole-network comparison.

S5     Alpha–CKA validation details
Pearson and Spearman correlations were calculated between pairwise raw Alpha dissimilarity and
hidden 1 − CKA at each sampled checkpoint. Confidence intervals were estimated by deleting com-
plete runs, rather than treating the many network pairs as independent. Correlations were also
calculated separately within and between dataset groups to reveal correlations induced by the group
separation itself.
    At E20, Alpha and CKA produced similarly strong dataset contrasts, but their pairwise associ-
ation depended on which pairs were included. The pooled Pearson correlation was 0.9269, fell to
0.6524 within datasets, and was only 0.1071 between datasets (Table S4). Thus, the high pooled
value mainly reflects agreement on the broad dataset grouping. Alpha and CKA are complementary
measurements and should not be treated as interchangeable rankings of individual network pairs.

S6     ReLU-rescaling stress test
Positive rescaling of a hidden neuron can multiply its incoming weights and divide its outgoing
weights without changing the represented function. We applied deterministic neuronwise rescalings
spanning progressively wider multiplicative envelopes. Raw Alpha was recalculated after rescaling,
while the canonical gauge was checked by comparing recovered weights elementwise. Logits and
predicted classes on the fixed probe set verified functional invariance.
    The transformations preserved every predicted class and changed logits by at most 4.26 × 10−14 ,
yet raw Alpha changed materially (Table S5). At the strongest envelope, the layer-balanced contrast
retained 64.7% of its original value but remained positive. Canonicalization reduced the maximum
weight difference between transformed and untransformed networks to 1.55 × 10−15 and yielded a
final layer-balanced contrast of 0.15901 [0.15535, 0.16266]. These checks preserve the qualitative
dataset result while showing that raw Alpha values are not intrinsic properties of the represented
function.

S7                               High-resolution SGD trajectories
The early-SGD experiment stored checkpoints densely from initialization through the first two epochs.
For every measure, onset was defined before inspection of the results as the first post-initialization
checkpoint whose complete run-level confidence interval for the between-minus-within contrast lay
above zero. The nonuniform step axis is displayed as log2 (s + 1) so that the earliest updates remain
visible.
    Dataset-dependent representation structure was detected after two updates, and every weight
measure crossed the same prespecified criterion by update four (Table S6). Canonical Alpha had an
onset contrast of 0.1322 [0.1072, 0.1571], while the CKA contrast at update two was 0.0860 [0.0655,
0.1066]. In contrast, the mean functional 90% point occurred at update 64. The result therefore
rejects the tested calibration-then-consolidation ordering. It does not establish a lazy-training or
neural-tangent-kernel regime, which would require direct linearization and kernel-stability tests.

S8      Matching trajectories and implementation checks
The unmatched order, PIF-Hungarian assignment, and exact one-hidden-layer Git Re-Basin assign-
ment were evaluated at every checkpoint. Here the PIF-Hungarian label refers to the candidate-
selected benchmark procedure documented in the main Methods: candidate selection uses parameter-
count-weighted Alpha, whereas the displayed canonical Alpha endpoint is layer-balanced. It is
distinct from the direct canonical-Alpha profile assignment in the principal analysis. Random-
renumbering tests permuted the neuron indices of the second network, repeated each matching,
and composed the inferred assignment back into the original indexing. Assignment overlap is the
fraction of hidden neurons assigned identically by PIF-Hungarian and Git Re-Basin.
    Git Re-Basin was strongest for every pooled E20 endpoint (Table S7). Its advantage was expected
for weight cosine, which is closely related to its objective, but it also extended to canonical Alpha and
activation correspondence. The two methods agreed on only 16.5% of assignments [15.7%, 17.3%],
showing that their objectives selected different correspondences. Both methods returned exactly the
same assignment after random neuron renumbering, and the resulting permutations preserved the
network function to numerical precision. These are implementation and equivariance checks, not
evidence that either assignment is a unique neuronal correspondence.

S9                                   Activation-based matching validation
Activation correspondence was measured on the common probe set after centring each hidden-neuron
activation vector. Because activations were not used in either weight-based assignment, the measure
provides an independent assessment of functional correspondence. Endpoint distributions use individ-
ual pairs descriptively; inferential intervals reported in the main text remain based on complete-run
deletion.
    PIF-Hungarian matching increased pooled activation correlation by 0.0812 [0.0780, 0.0844] rela-
tive to the unmatched order. The improvement was strongest within datasets, where all 135 pairs
improved and the mean correlation rose from 0.0655 to 0.3078. Among between-dataset pairs, the
mean increase was smaller and 69.7% of pairs improved. Git Re-Basin improved every pair, exceeded
PIF-Hungarian for every pair, and reached mean correlations of 0.4928 within and 0.1747 between
datasets. The authors’ procedure therefore has independently measurable functional value, but the
established baseline is clearly stronger.

it characterizes how much of the initialized sign pattern remains, but does not identify a causal

S11      Synthetic matching implementation sanity check
The iterative multilayer implementation was also checked in a deliberately favorable synthetic setting.
A random network with architecture 20 → 12 → 10 → 8 → 6 was copied, its three hidden layers were
permuted, and independent Gaussian noise with a standard deviation equal to 30% of that of each
weight matrix was added. The noisy copy was then aligned to the original with Euclidean, SND, or
Alpha profile costs. Matching reduced the corresponding full-network dissimilarities from 0.40174 to
0.08844, from 0.70260 to 0.26841, and from 0.44991 to 0.00008, respectively.
    This construction has a known ground-truth correspondence and therefore tests whether the code
can recover an imposed multilayer permutation in the presence of noise. It is not an independent
training experiment, does not validate the zig-zag procedure on separately optimized deep networks,
and does not support a claim of state-of-the-art matching. The activation-based comparisons of
independently trained networks in Fig. S9 provide the more relevant functional validation for the
empirical study.
